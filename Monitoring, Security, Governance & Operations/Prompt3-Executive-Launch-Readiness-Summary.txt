We evaluated two oversight designs: fully automated determinations, or confidence-banded human review. We chose a mandatory credit-officer checkpoint for every case scoring 0.48–0.55 and every low-confidence income extraction, because the jurisdiction requires explainable, human-reviewed decisions at defined points and 11% of staging cases fall in that band. The trade-off is reviewer workload and slower outcomes for those applicants, which we address with sized reviewer capacity, an explanation pack for each case and the extraction remediation in Section 1. We evaluated two defenses for applicant documents: relying on model robustness, or screening content with Prompt Shield before classification. We chose Prompt Shield because seven indirect-injection detections in one staging week show the vector is active. The trade-off is added latency in a chain already at 4.8 seconds p95, plus some legitimate documents quarantined, which we address with trace-led latency remediation and a manual review queue. We recommend a conditional launch, subject to four launch conditions, not a declaration of full readiness. Two residual risks remain. First, boundary-band review could slide into backlog or rubber-stamping; the CRO must approve the band, reviewer capacity and override tolerance. Second, a novel injection could evade Prompt Shield and alter extracted income; the CRO and Head of Technology must formally accept it. If either risk materializes, before or after launch, the workflow owner suspends automated determinations for the affected case type, a Severity 1 incident opens, and the CRO and Head of Technology are notified within one hour.
